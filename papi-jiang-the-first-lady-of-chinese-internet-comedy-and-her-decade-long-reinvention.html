Papi Jiang: The First Lady of Chinese Internet Comedy and Her Decade-Long Reinvention

Baby Zhao October 6, 2026

Papi Jiang, born Jiang Yilei on February 17, 1987, in Shanghai, is a Chinese actress, short-video creator, and one of the most enduring figures in the history of Chinese internet culture. She graduated from the Central Academy of Drama with both a bachelor's and a master's degree in directing, a background that distinguishes her from the vast majority of Chinese content creators who entered the field without formal training in performance or narrative construction. Her channel on Bilibili, where she is officially certified as a "2018 Top 100 UP Creator" and a "Renowned Comedy UP Creator," has accumulated over 9.4 million followers and more than 1.29 billion total views as of September 2026. But her significance extends far beyond any single platform metric. Papi Jiang is widely credited with legitimizing short-form comedy as a serious creative discipline in China, and her career trajectory, from a graduate student posting videos from her dormitory to a producer overseeing a content studio, offers a case study in how a creator can survive and thrive across multiple cycles of platform evolution.

Papi Jiang's path to internet fame was neither linear nor accidental. She grew up in Shanghai and attended the Shanghai No. 3 Girls' High School, where her saxophone talent in the school's girls' wind band earned her a twenty-point bonus on her high school entrance examination and a guaranteed place in the school's high school division. She was accepted into the Central Academy of Drama's directing program in 2005 and spent her undergraduate years actively involved in theater, both performing in and directing stage productions. After graduation, she explored several professional directions without finding a stable footing, and in 2013, at the age of twenty-six, she made the deliberate decision to return to the Central Academy of Drama for a master's degree in directing. It was during this period of graduate study that she began uploading short comedic videos to Chinese social media platforms, initially as a creative outlet rather than a career strategy. Her breakout came in 2015 and 2016, when a series of videos featuring her signature technique of accelerated voice modulation, rapid costume changes, and sharp observational humor about everyday life went viral across Weibo, WeChat, and Bilibili. By March 2016, she was being described by Chinese media as the "number one internet celebrity of 2016," and her account had attracted millions of followers within a matter of months.

What made Papi Jiang's content distinctive was not merely its humor but its structural sophistication. Unlike the loose, improvisational style that characterized much of Chinese short-form comedy at the time, her videos were tightly scripted, carefully paced, and built around a clear comedic premise that she would explore through multiple character perspectives. In a typical early video, she might play five or six different roles, each representing a recognizable social archetype—the anxious mother, the dismissive boyfriend, the overly competitive colleague—and use rapid cuts and voice changes to create the illusion of a conversation among them. This multi-role technique, combined with her background in directing, gave her videos a theatrical quality that set them apart from the confessional, talking-head format that dominated the platform. Her dialogue was colloquial and often self-deprecating, drawing on the anxieties and absurdities of urban Chinese life: the pressures of marriage and childbearing, the indignities of office politics, the gap between aspirational consumer culture and financial reality. She described herself in her videos with the now-iconic tagline, "I am Papi Jiang, a woman who combines beauty and talent," a line delivered with enough ironic distance to signal that she was fully aware of the absurdity of self-branding in the attention economy.

The commercial and cultural impact of Papi Jiang's early success was unprecedented. In March 2016, she received 12 million yuan in first-round financing from ZhenFund, Logic Show, and two other investment firms, with an estimated valuation of approximately 120 million yuan, making her the first internet celebrity in China to receive venture capital investment. In April of that year, the first commercial placement on one of her videos was auctioned for 22 million yuan, a figure that shocked observers and signaled that short-form video could be a viable advertising medium at scale. This auction is widely regarded as a watershed moment in the professionalization of Chinese content creation, demonstrating that individual creators could command advertising rates comparable to traditional media. In 2018, she was named to Bilibili's inaugural Top 100 UP Creators list, and in the same year, she was appointed Chief Content Officer of Baidu App, a role that placed her at the intersection of content creation and platform strategy. She also co-founded papitube, a multi-channel network focused on incubating and managing short-video creators, which at its peak represented dozens of signed talents and was recognized as one of China's leading MCN organizations.

Papi Jiang's creative output has evolved substantially over the past decade. In the late 2010s and early 2020s, she expanded into acting, appearing in films such as "The Truth About Beauty" and "Wish You Were Here," and taking a supporting role in Wong Kar-wai's acclaimed television series "Blossoms Shanghai," where she played Ling Hong, a boutique owner whose pragmatic, street-smart personality provided a counterpoint to the more romantic protagonists. Her performance was widely praised for its naturalism and comic timing, with critics noting that her background in directing gave her a nuanced understanding of how to build a character within an ensemble. In 2024, she made a significant structural decision: she stepped back from her management role at papitube and returned to full-time content creation, launching a new interview program called "Warm Welcome." The show's format is deliberately minimalist—one table, two chairs, no elaborate sets or scripted segments—and it centers on guests sharing personal recommendations for everyday products while engaging in unguarded conversation. The program became a breakout success, with over 70 million views on Bilibili and 300 million on Douyin, and it was credited with revitalizing the careers of several established entertainers who appeared as guests.

The broader significance of Papi Jiang's career lies in her demonstration that creative longevity on Chinese social media is possible through continuous reinvention rather than mere consistency. She has outlasted the vast majority of her contemporaries from the 2016 "internet celebrity" boom, many of whom faded within a few years. Her ability to move from short-form comedy to acting to interview-based content reflects a willingness to abandon formats that no longer serve her and to invest in new creative challenges even when the commercial incentives favor sticking with proven formulas. For Bilibili, she represents an early bridge between the platform's niche gaming and anime origins and its later emergence as a mainstream cultural force. For aspiring creators, her career demonstrates that a formal education in narrative craft can translate into meaningful advantages in a field that often valorizes spontaneity over discipline. And for the broader landscape of Chinese digital culture, she stands as a reminder that the most durable figures are not those who chase every trend but those who build a body of work that can accommodate growth and change.

In summary, Papi Jiang is not merely a successful content creator but a transitional figure who helped define what Chinese short-form video could be and then repeatedly redefined what her own career could become. Her decade-long presence at the center of Chinese internet culture is a testament to her creative instincts, her willingness to evolve, and her insistence on maintaining a degree of artistic control even as the commercial pressures around her intensified. She remains, in the most literal sense of her own famous tagline, a woman who combines beauty and talent—but more importantly, she combines talent with the discipline and adaptability required to sustain a creative life over the long term.
