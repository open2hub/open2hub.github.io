Zhiliaojieya Mengwu: The One-Man Studio Bringing the World's Most Overlooked Animals to Millions

Baby Zhao October 6, 2026

Zhiliaojieya Mengwu, whose real name is Peng Xianbo, is a Chinese wildlife content creator and one of the most distinctive voices in Bilibili's knowledge section. Operating as a completely independent one-man studio, he has built an audience of over 4.3 million followers on Bilibili as of January 2025, with his content focused on the natural stories of rare, unconventional, and frequently misunderstood animals. His channel name, which translates roughly to "Got It, Stress-Relief Cute Creatures," is deliberately playful, but the work itself is anything but frivolous. Peng has been selected for Bilibili's annual Top 100 UP Creators list for three consecutive years, from 2022 through 2024, and in 2025 he received the platform's "Annual Public Welfare UP Creator" award, a recognition that reflects the social mission embedded in his work. His videos have been described as a "stress-relief zone for graduate school entrance exam preparation" and "the Animal World for a new generation," a formulation that captures both their calming effect and their role as an accessible entry point into natural history.

Peng Xianbo's path to becoming one of Bilibili's most recognized wildlife communicators was neither planned nor straightforward. During the COVID-19 pandemic, he found himself with time on his hands and turned to video creation as a way to make a living. His initial forays into the platform were in the guichu or "ghost畜" genre, a style of remix video that was popular on Bilibili at the time but which had begun to decline in both viewership and monetization potential. Recognizing that the format was no longer sustainable, he made a strategic pivot based on his academic background: he had studied animal medicine in university, and he decided to apply that training to a new creative direction. The transition was not immediate or easy. In the early period, his monthly income was only one or two thousand yuan, insufficient to cover his basic expenses, and each video required five to six days of production, with scripts exceeding two thousand words and AI voiceover meticulously adjusted for quality. He has since described those first two years as a period of "stubbornly grinding it out," during which he could barely afford rent and had to rely on sheer persistence to continue.

What distinguishes Zhiliaojieya Mengwu from the vast field of animal content on Chinese video platforms is his deliberate focus on species that most creators overlook. Rather than featuring domestic pets or the charismatic megafauna that dominate traditional wildlife programming, he specializes in snakes, insects, and other creatures that are typically regarded with fear, disgust, or indifference. His videos present these animals through a documentary-style approach, combining carefully sourced footage with humorous and accessible narration that reframes them as fascinating rather than threatening. He describes his own work as "non-serious science communication," a phrase that signals both his rejection of academic solemnity and his commitment to accuracy. His content operates on two distinct tracks: one track uses curated internet footage to create light, entertaining science videos that introduce viewers to animal behaviors and biological facts, while the other track involves original field documentation, often produced in collaboration with conservation organizations such as the SEE Foundation, to record wildlife in China's nature reserves and national parks. The latter track has taken him to locations such as the Chongzuo Nature Reserve in Guangxi, where he documented the white-headed langur and other protected species as part of a public welfare initiative with the mobile game Azur Lane. The structural variety of his output allows him to serve audiences with different levels of engagement, from casual viewers seeking entertainment to those genuinely interested in conservation and biodiversity.

Among his most representative works are videos such as "The Jaguar: What Era Are We Living In," "Failure Means Becoming a Box: The Courtship Stories of Unpopular Animals," and "Animal World's Version of Three Heroes Fighting Lu Bu: A Pride of Lions Takes On a Nile Crocodile". These titles exemplify his distinctive approach to naming and framing: they draw on internet slang, classical literary references, and absurdist humor to make the subject matter immediately engaging, while the content itself remains grounded in accurate biological information. His video about the Tibetan fox, titled "As Long As the Green Hills Remain," uses the animal's distinctive facial expression, which has become a meme in Chinese internet culture, as a hook to discuss its habitat, behavior, and conservation status. Another characteristic work, "The Clouded Leopard: Living Fossils of the Forest," combines footage of the rarely seen predator with narration that explains its evolutionary history and the threats it faces. His field documentation in Guangxi, produced with the SEE Foundation and featuring voice actor Ge Ping as an "animal observer," represents his most ambitious production to date, blending original footage with conservation messaging and a narrative structure that positions viewers as witnesses to the work of protecting endangered species.

The platform achievements of Zhiliaojieya Mengwu are substantial and reflect the growing appetite for science content on Bilibili. In June 2022, his rare animal science videos reached the top of Bilibili's "dark horse" growth chart, with his account gaining 1.148 million followers in a single quarter and a single-day peak of 57,700 new followers. At that time, his total follower count was only around 200,000, meaning that his growth during that period represented a more than fivefold increase. By January 2025, his follower count had reached 4.331 million, and his content has continued to accumulate millions of views across his video library. His three consecutive years on the Top 100 UP Creators list, from 2022 through 2024, place him among a select group of knowledge creators who have achieved sustained recognition, and his 2025 "Annual Public Welfare UP Creator" award specifically acknowledged the conservation dimension of his work. His selection alongside creators such as Luo Xiang, Lao Fanqie, Muyu Shuixin, and MediaStorm on the 2024 Top 100 list underscores his position within the platform's most influential cohort.

The broader social value of Peng Xianbo's work lies in his demonstration that conservation communication can be genuinely popular without resorting to either alarmism or sentimentality. By focusing on species that are typically ignored or feared, he expands his audience's understanding of biodiversity beyond the narrow range of animals that receive mainstream attention. His collaboration with conservation organizations and his participation in public welfare initiatives, including the "E Qi Shou Hu Di Qiu" environmental short video program organized by the SEE Foundation, position him as a bridge between digital content creation and on-the-ground conservation efforts. For viewers who may never encounter these animals in person, his videos offer a form of vicarious natural history education that is both entertaining and ethically grounded. His insistence on operating as a one-man studio, handling scripting, production, and business negotiations himself, also represents a distinctive model of independent creative practice that stands apart from the team-based structures of many comparable channels.

In summary, Zhiliaojieya Mengwu is a creator whose significance lies in his ability to find wonder in the overlooked corners of the natural world. He has built a successful career not by chasing trends or adopting the formats of his competitors, but by applying his academic training and personal curiosity to subjects that others have neglected. His three consecutive years on Bilibili's Top 100 list, his 4.3 million followers, and his 2025 public welfare award are all evidence that this approach has resonated with a large and loyal audience. For Bilibili, he represents the knowledge section at its most accessible and its most purposeful. For viewers, he offers a reminder that the most rewarding discoveries are often found in places we have been taught not to look. And for the broader landscape of Chinese digital culture, his career is a testament to the possibility of building a sustainable creative practice on the foundations of patience, expertise, and a genuine commitment to sharing the world's strangest and most wonderful creatures with anyone willing to pay attention.
