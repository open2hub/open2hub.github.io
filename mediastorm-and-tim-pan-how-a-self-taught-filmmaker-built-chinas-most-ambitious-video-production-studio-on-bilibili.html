MediaStorm and Tim Pan: How a Self-Taught Filmmaker Built China's Most Ambitious Video Production Studio on Bilibili

Dabao Zhao October 6, 2026

MediaStorm, known in Chinese as Yingshi Jufeng, is a Chinese filmmaking and technology content studio founded and operated by Pan Tianhong, who is universally known by his English name, Tim. Born in 1996 in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, Tim is a graduate of the University of Kent in the United Kingdom, where he studied film. His path to becoming one of Bilibili's most recognized creators was neither linear nor conventional. By his own account and according to media profiles, he was a self-described "internet-addicted teenager" and a poor student during his early years, a young person whose parents and teachers had little reason to expect academic distinction. He left China after middle school to study abroad, eventually earning a degree in film. In 2014, while still a student, he created the MediaStorm account, and in 2015 he uploaded his first video to the internet. After returning to China in 2017, he assembled a small studio team and began producing content focused on filmmaking equipment reviews, technical tutorials, and original short films. What began as a one-person operation has since grown into a company with over 150 employees, annual revenue exceeding one hundred million yuan, and a multi-platform audience exceeding forty million followers, with more than seventeen million on Bilibili alone as of September 2026.

MediaStorm's creative identity is defined by an unusual combination of technical rigor and cinematic ambition. The studio's primary output falls into several overlapping categories: meticulous reviews of cameras, lenses, and production equipment; instructional content that teaches viewers the practical skills of filmmaking, from lighting and composition to editing and color grading; and original short films and documentary projects that demonstrate the techniques being taught. This tripartite structure, in which the studio both explains how filmmaking works and then produces work that exemplifies those principles, distinguishes MediaStorm from creators who specialize in only one mode. The production values of the channel are consistently exceptional; MediaStorm has been described by viewers and critics as "Bilibili's standard-bearer for image quality," a reputation earned through a commitment to shooting in high resolution, employing professional-grade equipment, and investing heavily in post-production. Tim's on-camera presence is similarly distinctive: he is articulate, technically fluent, and visibly enthusiastic about the craft of filmmaking, but he avoids the performative personality-driven style that characterizes many of Bilibili's most popular creators. His videos feel less like entertainment and more like an invitation into a professional workshop, where the viewer is treated as a serious participant rather than a passive spectator.

Among MediaStorm's most significant works is the 2020 documentary "Ascent: We Flew to 30,000 Meters Together," a project that involved launching a high-altitude balloon carrying professional cameras to capture 8K, 360-degree panoramic footage of the Earth's curvature and the near-space environment. The film was not merely a technical demonstration but a narrative about collective aspiration; the team invited viewers to submit photographs and wishes that were attached to the balloon, and the resulting footage was intercut with behind-the-scenes documentation of the preparation and the challenges encountered along the way. The project exemplified MediaStorm's approach to content: ambitious in scope, technically demanding, emotionally resonant, and fundamentally about the process of attempting something difficult rather than the polished result. Other representative works include "We Bought a Chinese Satellite," which documented the studio's collaboration with a domestic satellite manufacturer, and the short film "Little Garlic," which showcased the studio's narrative capabilities beyond technical documentation. The studio's 2023 collaboration with camera manufacturer Insta360 to launch a satellite for Earth imaging represented a further extension of this ambition, blending content creation with genuine technological experimentation.

MediaStorm's platform achievements are substantial and sustained. The channel has been recognized as a Bilibili Top 100 UP Creator for six consecutive years, from 2020 through 2025, and has received the platform's Highest Popularity Award for 2023 and its Diverse Innovation Award for 2022. As of September 2026, the Bilibili channel has 17.42 million followers, and the studio's total footprint across Bilibili, Douyin, Weibo, and Xiaohongshu exceeds forty million followers. The company's commercial performance is equally notable: MediaStorm reported annual revenue exceeding one hundred million yuan in 2024, with profits above ten million yuan, and a single branded T-shirt sold more than two hundred thousand units in a year. Tim was recognized as one of China's "Good Netizens" by the seventh China Youth Good Netizen program in 2024, and in 2025 he was named among Zhejiang Province's top ten "network science communicators". Beyond these formal recognitions, MediaStorm's influence is evident in its capacity to secure collaborations with major technology brands and its participation in high-profile public events, including a 2026 zero-degree snow survival livestream that accumulated 169 million views.

The broader social value of MediaStorm's work lies in its demonstration that technical education and artistic ambition are mutually reinforcing rather than opposed. Tim and his team have built a creative enterprise that treats filmmaking as both a craft to be taught and an art to be practiced, and in doing so they have helped raise the overall standard of video production on Bilibili. Their instructional content has been used by aspiring creators as a practical curriculum, while their original films have shown what is possible when those lessons are applied with sufficient resources and dedication. The studio's willingness to invest in projects with uncertain commercial returns, such as the high-altitude balloon documentary and the satellite collaboration, reflects a commitment to exploration that is rare in a content landscape often driven by algorithmic optimization. At the same time, MediaStorm has not been immune to criticism; observers have noted that the studio's technical strengths do not always translate into nuanced engagement with social or emotional subject matter, and a 2026 incident involving a livestream conflict and subsequent controversy over the studio's workplace culture required a public response from the company. These challenges notwithstanding, MediaStorm's trajectory from a solo YouTube-style channel to a full-fledged production company represents one of the most ambitious and sustained creative projects in the history of Chinese digital media.

In summary, MediaStorm and Tim Pan represent a model of the creator as studio builder, someone who began with a camera and an idea and gradually constructed an institution capable of executing projects that no individual could accomplish alone. Tim's personal story, from a self-described academic failure to the founder of a company with over a hundred employees and a nine-figure revenue, is a narrative about the value of pursuing a genuine passion with discipline and persistence. For Bilibili, MediaStorm is a cornerstone of the platform's aspiration to be a home for serious creative work, not merely entertainment. For aspiring filmmakers and content creators, the studio offers both a practical education and an aspirational vision of what a career in video production can become. And for the broader landscape of Chinese digital culture, MediaStorm is a reminder that the most enduring creative enterprises are those that treat their audience as collaborators in a shared project of exploration and craft.
