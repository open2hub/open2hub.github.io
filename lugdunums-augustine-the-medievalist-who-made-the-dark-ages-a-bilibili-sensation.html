Lugdunum's Augustine: The Medievalist Who Made the Dark Ages a Bilibili Sensation

Baby Zhao October 8, 2026

Lugdunum's Augustine, known simply as Augustine to his followers, is a Chinese history content creator and one of the most distinctive figures in Bilibili's knowledge section. His channel name is a layered reference: Lugdunum is the ancient Roman name for Lyon, the French city where he pursued his doctoral studies, and Augustine evokes both the Christian theologian Saint Augustine of Hippo and the intellectual tradition of medieval scholasticism. He is a native of Jinan, Shandong Province, born in the mid-1990s, and he holds a doctorate in medieval European history and church history from a French university. He describes himself with characteristic self-deprecation as "an old man telling stories under the banyan tree at the village entrance," a phrase that captures the disarmingly casual tone he brings to subjects that most Chinese audiences would consider forbiddingly obscure. His Bilibili channel has accumulated over one million followers, and in January 2026 he was named to the platform's annual Top 100 UP Creator list for 2025, a recognition that placed him alongside creators with audiences many times larger than his own.

Augustine's path to content creation was neither planned nor gradual. He began uploading videos to Bilibili in March 2022, while he was still living in France completing his doctoral research. In an interview with The Paper, he explained that he was encouraged by his family to try video creation, a suggestion he initially treated as a casual experiment rather than a career pivot. His first video, uploaded without professional equipment or a developed creative plan, accumulated over 2.6 million views, an extraordinary debut for a creator with no prior platform presence. The success was not the result of algorithmic luck or a viral gimmick. It was the consequence of a simple insight: Augustine possessed something that very few Chinese-language content creators had, which was genuine academic expertise in a subject that Chinese audiences had encountered only through Hollywood films, fantasy novels, and video games. Medieval Europe, with its cathedrals, crusades, feudal hierarchies, and papal politics, was a familiar imaginative landscape for Chinese viewers, but it was almost never explained by someone who had actually studied its primary sources, read its Latin and French documents, and understood its institutional complexities from the inside.

What distinguishes Augustine's content from the vast field of Chinese history communication is the combination of scholarly rigor and narrative accessibility that he brings to every subject. His videos are meticulously researched; he has stated that each video requires approximately two weeks of source analysis, scriptwriting, and editing, and that he consults primary documents in Latin and Old French alongside secondary scholarship in multiple languages. Yet the presentation is anything but academic. He speaks in a relaxed, conversational register, punctuating his explanations with humorous asides, rhetorical questions, and references to contemporary culture that make the material feel immediate rather than remote. His video titles often adopt a provocative or playful tone: "How Did the Pope Scheme and Survive in Medieval Europe?", "Was King Arthur a Real Person or a Literary Invention?", "Why Could Football Only Have Been Born in England?". These titles draw viewers in with the promise of a satisfying answer to a question they did not know they had, and the videos deliver on that promise by building arguments from evidence rather than simply recounting familiar legends. His video length averages around twenty-five minutes, and despite this duration, his content maintains unusually high completion rates, a metric that Bilibili founder Chen Rui cited in a 2026 speech as evidence that young users are willing to invest sustained attention in high-quality long-form knowledge content.

The core of Augustine's creative output is a series of interconnected video projects on medieval European history and the institutional development of the Catholic Church. The "Popes and Papal Power" series, which he has been releasing in installments since 2023, traces the evolution of the papacy from a marginal bishopric in Rome to the dominant political and spiritual authority of medieval Christendom. The series examines papal elections, the conflicts between popes and emperors, the Great Schism of 1054, and the institutional mechanisms through which the Church consolidated its power. A separate series on the Western Schism explores the period between 1378 and 1417 when rival claimants to the papacy divided Europe along political lines, a subject that Augustine treats not as an arcane ecclesiastical dispute but as a case study in how institutional legitimacy is contested and reconstructed. His videos on the Crusades examine the motivations of participants, the logistical realities of medieval warfare, and the long-term consequences of the encounters between Latin Christendom and the Islamic world. Beyond explicitly religious and political subjects, he has produced videos on the history of bread, the origins of aristocratic surnames, the development of medieval universities, and the cultural significance of courtly love. This range reflects his conviction that medieval history is not a narrow specialty but a lens through which to understand the foundations of modern European institutions, from legal systems and universities to the separation of church and state.

Augustine's platform achievements, while modest in absolute numbers compared to Bilibili's most popular entertainment creators, are significant in the context of knowledge content. His follower count surpassed one million in 2025, and his videos consistently generate hundreds of thousands of views, with his most popular works exceeding two million. In January 2026, he was named to Bilibili's 2025 Top 100 UP Creator list, a selection that was widely noted in media coverage as evidence of the platform's commitment to supporting niche intellectual content. The Paper described his inclusion as a signal that "young users' demand for deep knowledge and preference for high-quality long-form video" were reshaping the value system of Chinese content creation. His selection alongside creators like He Tongxue, Luo Xiang, and Fangstafu placed him in the company of Bilibili's most respected knowledge communicators, and his acceptance speech, in which he expressed surprise at the recognition, was received by his audience as characteristic of his unassuming public persona. In April 2026, Bilibili founder Chen Rui cited Augustine's channel in a keynote address at the China Network Audio-Visual Conference as an example of how the platform's community can support creators who begin with small audiences and grow through consistent, high-quality output.

The broader social value of Augustine's work lies in his demonstration that expertise is not a barrier to popularity but a foundation for it. He did not simplify medieval history into easily digestible anecdotes; he explained complex institutional dynamics with the same patience and precision he would bring to a scholarly lecture, and his audience followed him. For Chinese viewers who have encountered the Middle Ages primarily through fantasy fiction and historical dramas, his videos offer a corrective: a picture of the medieval world that is grounded in evidence, attentive to uncertainty, and honest about what historians do and do not know. His willingness to acknowledge the limits of the evidence, to present competing interpretations, and to explain why certain questions remain unresolved distinguishes his work from the confident pronouncements of popular history content. In a media environment where expertise is often dismissed as elitism and where confident assertion is frequently rewarded over careful reasoning, Augustine represents a counterexample: a scholar who has found a mass audience by doing what scholars are trained to do, which is to ask precise questions, evaluate evidence, and explain the reasoning behind every conclusion.

In summary, Lugdunum's Augustine represents a model of the creator as a public scholar, someone who has translated the skills and habits of academic research into a form that works on a video platform without sacrificing the intellectual standards of his discipline. His first video's remarkable success was not an anomaly but the beginning of a sustained project to bring medieval European history to an audience that had never been offered it in a serious form. His one million followers, his Top 100 UP Creator recognition, and his citation by Bilibili's founder as a model of quality content all reflect the judgment that his work has earned from both the platform and its users. For Bilibili, he is evidence that the knowledge section can support creators who take the long view, investing weeks in research for a single video rather than chasing the rapid turnover of trending topics. For viewers, he offers a reminder that the most rewarding educational experiences are often those that require patience and attention, and that a subject as distant and unfamiliar as medieval church history can become vividly alive in the hands of someone who genuinely understands it.
