Fangstafu: The Paleontologist Couple Who Made Extinct Creatures Feel Alive

Baby Zhao October 7, 2026

Fangstafu is a Chinese science communication channel operated by Tang Cheng and his wife Cai Chunlin, dedicated to paleontology, evolutionary biology, and the broader life sciences. The channel's name is a phonetic transliteration of the English phrase "fun stuff," reflecting its founders' conviction that scientific knowledge should be enjoyable, accessible, and emotionally resonant. Tang Cheng holds a doctorate from the Institute of Neuroscience at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, one of China's most prestigious research institutions, where his work focused on the neural mechanisms underlying animal behavior. Cai Chunlin, his wife and creative partner, manages the visual design, editing, and production aspects of the channel, creating the distinctive animated style that has become one of Fangstafu's most recognizable features. The couple began uploading videos to Bilibili in 2019, while Tang Cheng was still a doctoral student, and by 2020, after his graduation, they made the decision to pursue science communication as a full-time career rather than following the conventional academic path of postdoctoral research and university faculty positions. That decision, which Tang Cheng has described as a departure from what his academic training had prepared him for, has resulted in one of the most successful and critically respected science channels in China, with over 4.2 million followers on Bilibili as of early 2025 and a continuous presence on the platform's annual Top 100 UP Creator list since 2020.

The origin of Fangstafu's audience can be traced to a single video: "Anomalocaris: The Story of the First Hegemon," a thirty-minute animated documentary about a Cambrian-era predator that lived more than five hundred million years ago. The video, which combined rigorous paleontological research with a narrative structure that treated the ancient creature as a protagonist with a personality, ambitions, and a tragic destiny, accumulated millions of views and transformed the couple's hobby into a viable career. What made the video distinctive was not merely its subject matter, which had rarely been treated with such narrative seriousness in Chinese-language science communication, but its method. Tang Cheng approached the Anomalocaris not as a specimen to be described but as a character whose life could be reconstructed from fossil evidence. He explained the scientific debates surrounding the creature's anatomy, the competing hypotheses about its role in the Cambrian ecosystem, and the uncertainties that remain even after decades of research. The video did not simplify the science; it made the science feel urgent and dramatic by showing how paleontologists build knowledge from fragmentary evidence and how that knowledge changes as new fossils are discovered.

This commitment to narrative reconstruction has become Fangstafu's signature approach. The channel specializes in what its creators describe as "personified paleontology," a method in which extinct organisms are presented as characters whose behaviors, ecological roles, and evolutionary fates are explained through storytelling rather than through conventional lecture formats. The "Ghost Valley Chronicles" series, which focuses on the evolutionary history of vertebrates from fish to humans, represents the most ambitious expression of this approach. In one representative episode, the evolution of the mammalian ear is presented not as an anatomical transformation but as a story about the gradual repurposing of bones that once formed the jaw hinge of reptilian ancestors. The narrative follows the evidence: fossil intermediates, developmental biology, and comparative anatomy are woven together into a coherent account that respects scientific uncertainty while still providing a satisfying narrative arc. The series has been widely praised for its clarity and for its ability to make viewers feel that they are discovering the past alongside the researchers whose work is being described.

Beyond "Ghost Valley Chronicles," Fangstafu's content encompasses a wide range of topics within evolutionary biology and paleontology. The channel has produced episodes on the evolution of vision, the biology of parasites, the history of the domestication of plants and animals, and the ecological dynamics of mass extinction events. A recurring theme is the relationship between form and function: how the shape of a bone, the structure of an eye, or the arrangement of teeth can reveal what an organism ate, how it moved, and what pressures shaped its evolution. Tang Cheng's academic training in neuroscience informs his treatment of sensory biology, and several episodes have explored the evolution of the nervous system and the emergence of complex behavior. His wife Cai Chunlin's background in design and animation is evident in the visual style of the videos, which uses simple but expressive illustrations and animations to convey anatomical relationships and evolutionary sequences. The combination of scientific depth and visual clarity has made Fangstafu accessible to audiences ranging from university biology students to casual viewers with no formal scientific training.

The channel's institutional recognition has been substantial. Fangstafu received the Guokr Science Communication Contribution Award in 2020 and the Gezhi Science Communication Award as Creator of the Year in 2022. Tang Cheng was named one of Shanghai's "Science Stars" in 2025, a designation awarded by the municipal government to recognize outstanding contributions to public understanding of science. The channel has been selected for Bilibili's Top 100 UP Creator list in 2020, 2022, 2023, 2024, and 2025, a record of sustained recognition that places it among the platform's most consistently honored science accounts. In 2025, the "Ghost Valley Chronicles" series received the platform's Annual Original Program award, acknowledging the series' distinctive contribution to Bilibili's knowledge ecosystem. The couple has also collaborated with major brands and institutions, including a 2025 partnership with the mobile game Azur Lane for a public welfare initiative focused on mangrove conservation, which took them to Hainan Province to document wetland ecosystems.

The social value of Fangstafu's work lies in its demonstration that scientific rigor and popular accessibility are not competing objectives. Tang Cheng and Cai Chunlin have built a mass audience by treating their viewers as capable of understanding complex scientific ideas, provided those ideas are presented with sufficient clarity and narrative coherence. Their channel has filled a significant gap in Chinese-language science communication, which historically has been dominated by either technical academic content inaccessible to general audiences or superficial popularizations that sacrifice accuracy for entertainment. By occupying the space between these extremes, Fangstafu has helped establish a model for what serious science communication can look like on Chinese video platforms. For younger viewers, particularly those who may be considering careers in science, the channel offers a vision of scientific work as an ongoing process of discovery rather than a collection of settled facts. The couple's decision to leave conventional academic careers in order to pursue public communication full-time is itself a statement about the value they place on making science accessible, and their success suggests that this value is shared by millions of viewers.

In summary, Fangstafu represents a distinctive model of the creator as scientific storyteller. Tang Cheng and Cai Chunlin have combined advanced academic training with narrative skill and visual creativity to produce a body of work that is both scientifically rigorous and genuinely engaging. Their focus on paleontology and evolutionary biology, subjects that are often treated as remote and technical, has demonstrated that even the most ancient and unfamiliar forms of life can be made to feel immediate and meaningful when they are presented as characters in a story about survival, adaptation, and the long history of life on Earth. For Bilibili, Fangstafu is evidence that the platform's knowledge section can support creators who take intellectual risks and trust their audiences to follow. For the broader landscape of Chinese science communication, the channel is a reminder that the most effective educational content is often that which treats the audience not as passive recipients of information but as participants in the ongoing human project of understanding the natural world.
