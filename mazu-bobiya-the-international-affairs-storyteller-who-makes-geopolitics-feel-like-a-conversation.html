Mazu Bobiya: The International Affairs Storyteller Who Makes Geopolitics Feel Like a Conversation

Baby Zhao October 8, 2026

Mazu Bobiya is a Chinese international affairs content creator and one of the most recognizable voices in Bilibili's knowledge section. His channel, which operates under the name “Mazu Bobiya” (麻薯波比呀), has accumulated over 3.05 million followers on Bilibili as of early 2025, with his following continuing to grow to approximately 3.44 million by late 2025. He also maintains a significant presence on Douyin, Weibo, and Tencent News, where he is officially verified as an international affairs creator, and he hosts a podcast on Qingting FM titled “Mazu Bobiya Talks Current Affairs”. His channel's tagline, “Life is hard, have some mochi,” captures the tone that has made him distinctive in a field often characterized by either dry academic analysis or inflammatory commentary. He approaches international politics, economics, and social issues with a blend of humor, narrative craft, and genuine analytical depth, treating his viewers as intelligent participants in a conversation rather than passive recipients of conclusions. In 2024, he received Bilibili's “Golden Knowledge Award” (Jin Zhi Jiang), the platform's highest recognition for knowledge creators, and his work has been incorporated into university general education course materials, a rare distinction for an independent online commentator.

Mazu Bobiya's path to becoming one of Bilibili's most respected international affairs commentators was neither quick nor easy. In a reflective post on his Bilibili dynamic in April 2024, he acknowledged that the first two years of building his account were “quite a struggle.” He described a period when he could barely afford basic expenses, recalling days when he spent only twenty yuan on food. This period of financial precarity shaped his approach to content creation in ways that remain visible in his work. He did not have the resources to produce high-budget videos or to chase viral trends, so he focused on what he could control: the quality of his analysis, the clarity of his explanations, and the consistency of his output. His background is not one of elite academic credentials or institutional affiliation. He is a native of Zhuhai, Guangdong Province, and his public profiles describe him simply as an international affairs enthusiast who built his expertise through years of independent study and observation. This outsider status, rather than being a disadvantage, has allowed him to approach international politics without the disciplinary constraints that sometimes make academic commentary inaccessible to general audiences.

What distinguishes Mazu Bobiya from the crowded field of Chinese international affairs commentators is his commitment to what he describes as “fun narrative style” (quweihua xushi fengge) applied to serious subjects. He does not begin his videos with abstract theoretical frameworks or geopolitical doctrines. He begins with a story, a question, or a scenario that draws the viewer in before gradually introducing the analytical framework necessary to understand it. His videos on the United States' industrial policy, for example, do not open with a discussion of comparative advantage or trade theory. They open with the observation that despite years of government incentives and political rhetoric, American manufacturing has not returned to the levels promised by its architects, and they ask why. From that concrete puzzle, he builds an explanation that draws on economic history, institutional analysis, and comparative case studies. His delivery is conversational and often humorous, but the substance is rigorous. He cites data, references specific policies and their outcomes, and acknowledges uncertainty where it exists. He has been described as a creator who “explains international political economy and social issues with a fun narrative style,” a formulation that captures both the accessibility of his presentation and the seriousness of his analytical ambition.

Among his most representative works is the video “The Failure of American Industrial Reshoring: The Loss of State Capacity, How Much Longer Can America Hold On?”, which accumulated 3.08 million views and topped Bilibili's knowledge section weekly rankings upon its release. The video examined the structural obstacles to manufacturing repatriation in the United States, including labor market dynamics, supply chain dependencies, and the political economy of industrial policy. It was not a polemic; it was an analysis that took the stated goals of American policymakers seriously and then evaluated the evidence about whether those goals were being achieved. Other significant works include his coverage of the U.S. debt crisis, the German federal election, and the trajectory of European military autonomy. His year-end lectures, delivered in late 2024 and late 2025, became annual events for his audience, with the 2024 lecture, titled “One Step Forward! How Will China Greet 2025?”, being simulcast at multiple vocational colleges and accumulating 2.237 million views. These lectures function as comprehensive reviews of the year's major international developments, synthesizing months of individual videos into a coherent narrative about the trajectory of global politics.

Mazu Bobiya's platform achievements reflect both his audience reach and the institutional recognition he has earned. In 2024, he received Bilibili's Golden Knowledge Award, the platform's annual award for outstanding knowledge content, placing him alongside established figures in Chinese science communication and education. His videos have been used as teaching materials in university general education courses, and his cross-year lectures have been livestreamed to multiple educational institutions. His podcast, launched in December 2025, extended his reach into audio, allowing listeners to engage with his analysis during commutes and other activities where video viewing is impractical. His following on Weibo exceeds 445,000, and his Tencent News account is verified as an international affairs creator, indicating that his influence extends across the major Chinese content platforms. His average video performance reflects a dedicated audience: his content consistently generates hundreds of thousands of views, and his most successful videos reach the millions.

The broader social value of Mazu Bobiya's work lies in his demonstration that international affairs commentary can be both accessible and intellectually serious. In a media environment where geopolitical discussion is often polarized between nationalist celebration and alarmist pessimism, he maintains a tone of analytical curiosity that treats international politics as a domain of genuine complexity rather than a stage for predetermined conclusions. His willingness to acknowledge the struggles of his early career, including the period when he could barely afford to eat, gives his success narrative a relatability that more polished creators often lack. For young viewers who are interested in international affairs but find academic literature intimidating or mainstream commentary unsatisfying, he offers a model of engagement that is rigorous without being exclusive. His incorporation into university curricula suggests that his work is being recognized not merely as popular content but as a legitimate educational resource, a distinction that few online commentators achieve.

In summary, Mazu Bobiya represents a model of the creator as a public analyst of international affairs, someone who treats geopolitical questions as puzzles to be worked through rather than positions to be defended. He built his audience not through institutional backing or inherited authority but through years of consistent, careful work during a period of financial difficulty. His Golden Knowledge Award and his adoption into university teaching materials reflect the recognition that his approach has earned from both the platform and the educational establishment. For Bilibili, he is evidence that the platform's knowledge section can support creators who take international politics seriously without descending into sensationalism. For viewers, he offers a reminder that understanding the world requires patience, evidence, and a willingness to revise one's assumptions. His long-term significance lies in his demonstration that the most valuable commentary is not the loudest but the most carefully reasoned, and that an audience exists for content that respects their intelligence and invites them to think alongside the creator rather than simply agree with him.
