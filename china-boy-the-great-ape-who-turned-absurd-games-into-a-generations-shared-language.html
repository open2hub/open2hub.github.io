China BOY: The "Great Ape" Who Turned Absurd Games into a Generation's Shared Language

Baby Zhao October 7, 2026

China BOY, whose real name is Wang Hanzhe, is a Chinese gaming commentator, live streamer, and one of the most enduring figures in Bilibili's game section. Born on June 2, 1998, in Guiyang, Guizhou Province, he is known across the platform by his full handle, China BOY Super Gorilla, a name that has become so synonymous with his persona that most viewers simply call him "China BOY" or "the Gorilla". His life story is one of unusual early independence and sustained self-direction. His mother passed away in December 2007, when he was nine years old, and he was subsequently raised by his grandmother. He began producing game commentary videos in 2012, at the age of fourteen, using little more than a mobile phone and a rudimentary understanding of video editing. After completing high school in 2016, he made the decision not to attend university, concluding that video creation offered a viable and meaningful career path. That same year, he moved alone from Guiyang to Shanghai to pursue content creation full-time, a decision that placed him among the earliest cohort of Chinese creators to treat online video as a profession rather than a hobby. As of late 2025, his Bilibili channel has over 10.5 million followers, and his total reach across Bilibili, Douyin, and other platforms exceeds twenty million.

China BOY's content is defined by a deliberate and consistent refusal to engage with the games that dominate mainstream gaming media. He rarely features competitive esports titles or AAA blockbusters. Instead, his videos are built around what Chinese internet culture calls "shadiao games," a term that translates roughly as absurdist, deliberately low-quality, or bizarrely conceived games that most creators would never consider covering. He has described himself as one of the people in China who has played the greatest number of these games, and the claim is credible. His library includes simulations of mundane occupations, horror games built almost entirely around jump scares, and independent titles that are technically crude but conceptually strange. His commentary transforms these games into comedic material through a combination of exaggerated reactions, absurdist commentary, and a voice that fans have described as one of the most distinctive in Chinese gaming content. The humor is not cruel; he treats even the worst games with a kind of affectionate curiosity, approaching them as artifacts of human creativity rather than as products to be evaluated. This stance distinguishes him from reviewers who approach games as consumers and from entertainers who treat bad games as targets for mockery. For China BOY, a strange game is an invitation to explore, and his willingness to take that invitation seriously is what makes his content compelling.

Among his most influential works is the "China BOY Commentary" series, which has been running in various forms since his earliest years on the platform. The series encompasses hundreds of videos across a wide range of games, and its longevity reflects the consistency of his creative voice. The 2013 series "Games Are Really Weird" established the template that he would refine over the following decade: a game that appears ordinary or poorly made is played with genuine curiosity, and the comedy emerges from the gap between the game's ambitions and its execution. The "Wang Xiansheng, Your Ghost Videos" series, in which he reacts to fan-made remixes and compilations of his own content, became one of his most popular recurring formats in the life section. His collaboration videos with other prominent Bilibili creators, particularly those in the loose collective sometimes referred to as the "Shanghai help group," including Lao Fanqie, Mou Huanjun, and Hu Shaobei, generated some of the platform's most-watched collaborative content. In 2022, he extended his creative reach into game development itself with the release of "A Piece of Poo" on Steam, a game in which players control a piece of excrement on a journey of self-improvement and combat, which accumulated hundreds of reviews and demonstrated his willingness to participate in the culture he had spent a decade commenting on.

China BOY's platform achievements are substantial and sustained. He has been selected for Bilibili's Top 100 UP Creator list in multiple years, including 2018, 2019, 2020, and 2023, a record of recognition that spans the platform's most formative period of growth. In 2018, he received Bilibili's Annual Danmaku Popularity Award, a recognition specifically tied to the interactive comment system that is central to the platform's culture. In 2019, he was named one of Tencent's Game Persons of the Year. His channel consistently ranks among the most-followed gaming accounts on Bilibili, and his average video performance reflects a loyal and engaged audience rather than a purely algorithmic one. Beyond Bilibili, he has maintained a significant presence on Douyin, where he is a signed game livestreamer, and he has been recognized as a contracted artist under Chao Dian Culture, one of the platform's major creator management agencies. In 2021, he participated in the "Long Scroll Treasure Hunt" project, a Bilibili initiative in which prominent creators introduced historical and cultural landmarks, and he served as the guardian of key revolutionary sites in the Yangtze River Delta region, including the First National Congress of the Communist Party of China Memorial.

The broader social significance of China BOY's career lies in his demonstration that sustained success in Chinese digital media does not require formal credentials, institutional backing, or conformity to the expectations of the gaming industry. He left school at eighteen and built a career through a combination of creative instinct, relentless output, and an intuitive understanding of what his audience found funny. His decision to focus on games that no one else would cover created a niche that he has occupied almost entirely on his own for over a decade. For young viewers who see their own interests as marginal or unserious, his work offers validation: the strange, the absurd, and the deliberately low-quality can be treated with the same attention and care that mainstream culture reserves for prestige objects. His movement from game commentary into life vlogs, music, writing, and game development reflects a creative restlessness that has allowed him to remain relevant as platform trends have shifted. In 2026, he published a book titled "Let It Rain, Make a Friend," extending his presence into yet another medium, and his recognition as a "Z-generation China BOY" by state media for his ability to "tell China's story with sincerity" suggests that his cultural influence has been acknowledged beyond the platform's own ecosystem.

In summary, China BOY represents a model of the creator as an intuitive cultural archivist of the absurd. He has spent more than a decade documenting the strangest corners of the gaming world, and in doing so he has built one of the most durable and recognizable personas on Bilibili. His career began with a fourteen-year-old filming himself playing games on a phone; it has since encompassed live streaming, music, writing, game development, and collaborations with some of the platform's most prominent figures. He has been selected for the Top 100 UP Creator list four times, received the platform's danmaku popularity award, and maintained a following of over ten million people through periods of significant change in Bilibili's content ecosystem. For Bilibili, he is a foundational figure in the game section, someone who helped define what gaming commentary could be in the platform's early years and who has continued to evolve alongside it. For viewers, he is a reminder that the most enduring creative work is often the least prestigious, and that a genuine interest in the strange and overlooked can be a foundation for a career that lasts.
